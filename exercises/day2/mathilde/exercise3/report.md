All distributions were generated by hand-coded inversion / Box-Muller and compared

## Part 2: Pareto Moments

| k    | E[X] simulated | E[X] theory | Var simulated | Var theory |
|------|----------------|-------------|---------------|------------|
| 2.05 | 1.9452         | 1.9524      | 9.96          | 37.19      |
| 2.5  | 1.6479         | 1.6667      | 1.44          | 2.22       |
| 3.0  | 1.5042         | 1.5000      | 0.68          | 0.75       |
| 4.0  | 1.3373         | 1.3333      | 0.22          | 0.22       |

**Problem at k = 2.05:** The theoretical variance is β²k / ((k−1)²(k−2)).
With k − 2 = 0.05 the denominator is tiny, giving Var ≈ 37. The sample
variance (≈ 9.96) severely underestimates this because the heavy tail
(power-law exponent close to 2) means extremely large values are rare but
dominate the true variance; a sample of n = 10,000 misses most of them.
For k ≤ 2 the variance is theoretically infinite — no finite sample can
estimate it reliably.

## Part 3: Normal Confidence Intervals

100 independent datasets of n = 10 from N(0, 1). 95% CIs constructed using
t and χ² critical values.

| CI type  | Coverage | Expected |
|----------|----------|----------|
| Mean     | 94 / 100 | ~95      |
| Variance | 96 / 100 | ~95      |

Both coverages are consistent with the nominal 95% level (binomial SE ≈ 2.2
intervals). The mean CI based on the t-distribution is exact; the variance CI
based on χ² is also exact under normality. With only n = 10 observations the
intervals are wide — a common feature of small-sample estimation.

## Part 4: Pareto via Composition

The Pareto distribution can be written as a Gamma-Exponential mixture:
draw Λ ~ Gamma(shape=k, scale=1/β), then X = β + Exp(rate=Λ).

For k = 3.0, β = 1:
- KS vs. theoretical Pareto: stat = 0.0060, p = 0.860 ✓
- KS vs. inversion samples: stat = 0.0109, p = 0.592 ✓

The composition sampler produces the same distribution as inversion — both
histograms align with the analytical pdf. Composition is conceptually useful
when the CDF cannot be inverted analytically; for Pareto, inversion is simpler.
